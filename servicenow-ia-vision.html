Design Principles

Five rules that govern every IA decision

These principles are the filter through which every structural choice, naming decision, and content placement should be evaluated.

Principle 01
🎯

Buyer-first, product-second

The IA presents ServiceNow through the lens of buyer roles and problems — not through ServiceNow's product line. A CIO navigating for IT resilience should find their path before they encounter a product name.

Principle 02
📐

One primary action per context

Every page has a single primary CTA appropriate to the buyer's stage. Awareness pages drive demo requests. Consideration pages drive product exploration. No page competes with itself.

Principle 03
📣

Proof at the point of consideration

Customer evidence, analyst recognition, and ROI data appear at the exact moment a buyer is evaluating a product or solution — not in a separate case study library they have to navigate to.

Principle 04
⚙️

Scalable by design

The IA accommodates new products, announcements, partner content, and audience segments without structural surgery. New nodes attach to existing templates. Announcements have a dedicated system — not an ad hoc blog post.

Principle 05
🔁

Serve all lifecycle stages simultaneously

New buyers, returning evaluators, and current customers coexist on the same site. The IA surfaces different entry points, content depths, and CTAs based on context — without building three separate sites.

How to use these principles: When any structural debate arises — where to put a new product, how to label a nav item, whether to gate content — run it through these five filters. A decision that fails Principle 01 (buyer-first) or Principle 02 (one primary action) should not ship regardless of internal stakeholder pressure.

Content System

Five scalable page templates

Every page on servicenow.com should map to one of these five templates. Templates enforce consistency, reduce editorial overhead, and ensure that new products, solutions, and announcements can be launched without structural surgery.

🏠
T1 — Homepage
1 page · All audiences · Refresh quarterly
HeroPlatform statement (outcome-specific) + Watch Demo CTA + Trust anchor (Fortune 500 stat + Gartner)
Routes5 persona quick-route chips: For IT / HR / Finance / CX / Developers
AI Story2-section scroll: Now Assist in action + Agentic AI concept teaser
ProofLogo strip with named outcomes (3–5 stats) + 1 featured customer story
NewsWhat's New preview (3 latest cards, link to full hub)
CTAPrimary: Watch Demo · Secondary: Contact Sales
🗂️
T2 — Solution Hub
10+ pages · Role + Industry · Annual refresh
HeroRole/industry-specific problem statement + primary outcome stat
Use Cases3 strategic use cases with linked product pages and outcome callouts
Proof1 featured peer customer story (same role/industry) + 2 logo callouts
ProductsRecommended product shortlist (3–5 max) with one-line descriptions
RecognitionRelevant analyst badge (Gartner, Forrester) for this domain
CTAPrimary: Talk to a [Role] Expert · Secondary: See All Customer Stories
📦
T3 — Product Page
15+ pages · Per-product · Semi-annual refresh
HeadlineOutcome-first (e.g., "Resolve IT incidents 40% faster with AI-powered ITSM")
Summary2–3 sentence plain-language description. No jargon. Buyer-readable.
Capabilities3 feature groups, each with outcome framing (not feature-list format)
DemoEmbedded product demo or screenshot walkthrough — ungated
Proof1 named customer quote + 1 outcome stat (inline, not linked away)
AI CalloutHow Now Assist enhances this specific product
CTAPrimary: See It in Action · Secondary: Download Product Brief (ungated)
📢
T4 — Announcement Card
Continuous · All product launches · Scalable
TitleAnnouncement headline — outcome-forward ("Now Assist now available for HR Service Delivery")
TagsProduct Area · Release Version · Date · Audience (Customer / Evaluator)
Summary3 sentences. What changed, why it matters, who it affects.
Links→ Product page · → Release notes (docs) · → Webinar / event (if applicable)
SubscribeEmail/RSS subscribe prompt on every card. Product-specific subscription optional.
🧪
T5 — Developer Content
Developer Hub · Technical audience · Always current
Tone Peer-to-peer. Technical. Minimal marketing language. Assume competence — don't over-explain.
Required Elements Code samples · API endpoint examples · Integration templates · Community links · "What you can build" showcase
CTA Primary: Activate Free Sandbox · Never: "Contact Sales" as a primary action on a developer page
Scalability note: When ServiceNow launches a new product (e.g., ServiceNow CRM in 2025), it should slot directly into T3 (Product Page), get an Announcement Card in T4, and be linked from the relevant Solution Hub (T2) within the same sprint. No new template needed. No IA restructuring required.
New IA Concepts

Three sections that don't exist today

These are the highest-leverage structural additions to the IA — each closing a specific gap in the current site that is costing ServiceNow buyers, developers, or customers.

★ New Section

Platform Hub

A dedicated editorial destination for ServiceNow's AI platform story — separate from the product catalog. Tells the Now Assist → Agentic AI → AI Control Tower narrative as a sequence, not a grid of product cards.

Why It Doesn't Exist Today

ServiceNow's AI products live as separate pages with no editorial thread connecting them. Buyers — especially technical evaluators — can't form a coherent mental model of how the AI stack works together.

What It Contains
Interactive AI architecture diagram (platform → AI layer → agents → governance)
Now Assist, Agentic AI, AI Control Tower as a connected narrative sequence
Unified Data Model explainer (key differentiator vs. Salesforce)
AI Control Tower live preview (screenshot or simulated dashboard)
Security, compliance, and governance callout for regulated enterprise buyers
Competitive parity: Salesforce has "The Agentforce Platform." Google has "Vertex AI Platform." ServiceNow needs an equivalent editorial destination.
★ New Section

What's New Hub

A scalable, filterable product announcement system that replaces ad hoc blog posts as the primary communication channel for product news — serving current customers and active evaluators simultaneously.

Why It Doesn't Exist Today

Product announcements are buried in the Workflow blog, mixed with thought leadership content, and impossible to filter by product. Current customers have no reliable way to track what's changed in the products they own.

What It Contains
Product Announcements Hub — filterable by product, date, release version, audience
Release Highlights — digestible summaries of each platform release
Knowledge Conference section — recordings, slides, and announcement summaries
Innovation Preview — editorial forward-look (not a product roadmap, an editorial perspective)
Product-specific email/RSS subscription (granular — subscribe to ITSM updates only)
Retention value: Current customers who return regularly for product news are the most likely to expand. This section turns the marketing site into a retention asset.
★ New Section

Developer Hub

A dedicated destination for developers and low-code builders — complete with a free sandbox, API reference, Creator Workflows showcase, and a developer community front-door. Build before you buy.

Why It Doesn't Exist Today

Developers and builders — who have increasing influence over enterprise platform decisions — are not treated as a first-class audience on servicenow.com. Creator Workflows and App Engine content is buried in the Products nav.

What It Contains
Developer Hub landing — tone: technical, peer-to-peer, zero marketing speak
Free Sandbox — email-only signup, pre-loaded with sample workflows, 30-day access
App Engine Studio showcase — video + interactive capability preview
Creator Workflows use-case library (searchable, tagged by complexity)
Full API Reference + integration samples in GitHub-linked repos
Developer Community front-door (rankings, questions, contributions)
Competitive parity: Google AI Studio (free), Salesforce Developer Edition (free), Stripe docs. ServiceNow has no equivalent free entry point for technical evaluators.
Scalability Model

Built to grow without structural surgery

Every IA decision was made with the question: "What happens when ServiceNow launches a new product, enters a new industry, or announces a major platform update?" The answer must always be: "It slots in. No redesign required."

Pillar 01

New Products → T3 Template

Every new ServiceNow product (e.g., ServiceNow CRM, Field Service AI expansion) deploys directly into the Product Page template (T3). It gets tagged to a domain group, linked from relevant Solution Hubs, and announced via the What's New Hub.

Launch effort: 1–2 weeks (content + QA) · No IA change required
Pillar 02

New Industries → T2 Template

When ServiceNow wants to expand coverage into a new vertical (e.g., Energy & Utilities, Retail), a new industry Solution Hub (T2) is created and linked from the Solutions nav. Customer Stories are filtered to that industry. No nav restructuring required.

Launch effort: 3–4 weeks (strategy + content + design) · Slot into existing Solutions nav
Pillar 03

Announcements → T4 Template

Product announcements, release updates, and Knowledge Conference news are published via the Announcement Card template (T4) into the What's New Hub. No editorial judgment needed about where it lives — the template and tagging system handles routing.

Launch effort: Same-day publishing · Filterable immediately on launch

What Doesn't Change

The primary navigation structure (Platform, Products, Solutions, Customers, Resources, What's New, Developers)
The five page templates (T1–T5) — these are the system's backbone
The four audience personas and their primary entry points
The utility navigation (Sign In, Watch Demo, Contact Sales)
The design system tokens (colors, typography, spacing, component library)

What Can Always Expand

Products: Any new product gets a T3 page and a domain grouping tag
Industries: New verticals add a T2 Solution Hub and a Customer Stories filter tag
Announcements: Unlimited via T4 — tagged, filterable, searchable
Personas: New audience types (e.g., Legal Ops, Procurement) add a Solution Hub and a persona route chip
Partner content: Partner spotlights, co-sell pages, and integration listings can live in Products (Build & Extend) or Customers without structural change
Governance recommendation: Assign a dedicated IA owner (Digital Experience / Web Strategy team) with authority to enforce template usage, approve new nav additions, and maintain the taxonomy. Without governance, the IA degrades over time as teams add one-off pages that don't follow the template system.
